# ALCOHOL AND BRAIN

**Status:** evidence collection template

## Scope

This section focuses on how alcohol exposure relates to brain function and structure, while distinguishing acute effects, chronic heavy exposure, alcohol use disorder, developmental exposure and recovery.

NIAAA describes alcohol as interfering with brain communication pathways and notes effects on balance, memory, speech and judgment. Its neuroscience resource describes effects of chronic heavy drinking on circuits involved in motivation, memory, decision-making, impulse control, attention and sleep regulation, including disruption of prefrontal cortical function in alcohol use disorder. citeturn0search0turn0search2

## Evidence separation

The atlas should distinguish:

**ACUTE FUNCTIONAL EFFECT**  
What happens during or shortly after exposure.

**CHRONIC ASSOCIATION / CHANGE**  
What is observed with repeated or heavy exposure.

**AUD-RELATED CHANGE**  
Findings specifically associated with alcohol use disorder.

**DEVELOPMENTAL EFFECT**  
Findings in developing brains.

**RECOVERY**  
Evidence concerning change after reduced exposure or abstinence.

NIAAA reports that some alcohol-related brain changes may partially reverse with abstinence; the atlas must preserve the specific population and conditions of the supporting study rather than generalize recovery to every individual. citeturn0search6

## Methodological rule

Do not use:

> «Алкоголь уничтожает участок мозга».

Prefer:

> «Исследования при таких условиях обнаруживали изменение X в области Y».

Then show:

**population → exposure → method → result → limitation → source**
