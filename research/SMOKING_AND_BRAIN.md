# SMOKING AND BRAIN

## Evidence layer

This document is the focused neuroscience layer for the PERCEPTION ATLAS.

### 1. Brain structure

A UK Biobank analysis of 33,293 participants found associations between smoking and lower total and regional gray-matter volume. Greater lifetime exposure was associated with lower total gray-matter volume, while longer abstinence among former smokers was associated with larger total gray-matter volume. The study is observational and does not by itself establish that every structural difference was caused by smoking.

### 2. Structural and functional networks

A 2025 systematic review and meta-analysis combined 25 VBM studies and 35 resting-state fMRI studies and reported structural and functional differences involving frontal, temporal, anterior cingulate, thalamic and other regions, as well as abnormalities across default-mode, executive-control and salience networks. The literature remains heterogeneous, so individual findings must be tagged to their specific populations and methods.

### 3. Electrical activity

A 2025 systematic review of EEG studies examined chronic tobacco smoking and reported heterogeneous changes in electrical brain activity. The atlas should therefore display EEG findings as a separate evidence layer rather than translating them into a simple «brain damage» label.

### 4. Regions for the interactive map

- **Prefrontal cortex** — executive functions and cognitive control.
- **Anterior cingulate cortex** — monitoring, conflict processing and cognitive control.
- **Insula** — interoceptive and salience-related processing.
- **Thalamus** — relay/integration functions; a prominent structural association in the UK Biobank analysis.
- **Hippocampal system** — learning and memory; evidence must be tagged by exposure and population.
- **Striatal / basal-ganglia systems** — reward, reinforcement and dependence-related circuitry.
- **Cerebellum** — motor and broader cognitive functions; findings vary by study.

The visual map must not imply that every listed region is «damaged» in every smoker.

### 5. Interactive evidence card

**REGION → FUNCTION → FACTOR → MECHANISM → OBSERVED FINDING → EVIDENCE LEVEL → CAUSALITY → POPULATION → METHOD → LIMITATIONS → WHAT IT DOES NOT PROVE → SOURCE**

### 6. Recovery

Recovery is a separate timeline, not a binary «reversible / irreversible» label. The UK Biobank study found an association between longer abstinence and larger gray-matter volume among former smokers. This does not establish that all smoking-related brain changes reverse or that every person follows the same trajectory.

### 7. Scientific language

Prefer «associated with», «observed in», «consistent with» and «evidence suggests» over «destroys», «kills brain cells» or «irreversibly damages the brain», unless a specific source establishes the stronger claim for the exact exposure and population.

### 8. Creative cognition

Creativity may be an exploratory functional layer, but not a single brain-region outcome. A 2015 review discusses possible relationships between psychostimulants, including nicotine, and particular stages of creative work. This is not evidence that smoking determines individual creativity.

### 9. Programmer view

**PROGRAMMER MODE** is an interface filter, not a medical conclusion.

It can connect:

**attention → working memory → executive control → cognitive flexibility → learning → error monitoring**

to corresponding evidence records.

There is insufficient direct evidence to claim that smoking itself determines programming performance. Data from cannabis intoxication must not be used as evidence for tobacco.

## Core rule

**The map visualizes evidence; it does not visualize a verdict.**
