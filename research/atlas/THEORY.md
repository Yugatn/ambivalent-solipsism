# PERCEPTION ATLAS — Theoretical Core

## The Atlas as a map of relations

PERCEPTION ATLAS is not a list of diseases and not a «harm calculator».

Its basic object is the relation between levels of description:

```
EXPOSURE
   ↓
BIOLOGICAL MECHANISM
   ↓
BODY SYSTEM
   ↓
ORGAN / REGION
   ↓
FUNCTION
   ↓
OBSERVABLE OUTCOME
   ↓
EVIDENCE
```

At every arrow the question is:

> **What exactly is known, how was it established, and what remains unknown?**

## Population ≠ individual

A population association describes a distribution across people.

It does not by itself predict the future of one particular subject.

This distinction is essential for cognitive safety.

## Brain ≠ collection of isolated modules

Complex functions arise from interactions among distributed neural systems.

Therefore:

> «participates in» is preferred to «controls».

## Risk ≠ destiny

A statistical increase in risk is not a personal diagnosis.

Atlas should never convert a probability distribution into an identity statement.

## Recovery ≠ reset

When evidence indicates recovery or plasticity, the timescale and limits must be specified.

«Recoverable» and «irreversible» are both claims requiring evidence.

## The subject remains a subject

The person represented by biological data is not reducible to the data.

The Atlas describes measurable processes while preserving the distinction between:

```
DATA
PERSON
INTERPRETATION
```

## AS interpretation

Within Ambivalent Solipsism, the Atlas can be understood as a transparent interface between:

- subject;
- perception;
- scientific model;
- other subjects;
- residual uncertainty.

The scientific layer remains independently testable.
