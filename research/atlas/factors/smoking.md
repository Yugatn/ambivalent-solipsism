# Smoking and Nicotine — Atlas Node

## Definition
The Atlas separates tobacco smoking from nicotine exposure.

Smoking involves a complex mixture generated by combustion. Nicotine is one component and has its own pharmacology.

Therefore:

```
nicotine ≠ tobacco smoke ≠ smoking
```

## Exposure dimensions
- cigarettes/day;
- duration;
- dependence;
- inhalation pattern;
- combustible versus non-combustible exposure;
- second-hand exposure.

## Brain
The smoking literature contains evidence of associations with cognitive measures and structural brain differences, but interpretation requires attention to confounding, dependence and exposure definition.

## Methodological rule
Evidence about nicotine alone cannot automatically be used as evidence about cigarette smoking.

Evidence about adolescents, prenatal exposure or passive smoke must not automatically be generalized to adult active smokers.

## Creativity and programming
The Atlas will not claim that smoking either «improves» or «destroys» creativity or programming ability without task-specific evidence.

Programming productivity is a complex outcome influenced by sleep, dependence, withdrawal, environment and many non-neural variables.

## Unknown
Direct evidence linking tobacco smoking to real-world programming productivity remains much thinner than evidence concerning specific cognitive tasks.
