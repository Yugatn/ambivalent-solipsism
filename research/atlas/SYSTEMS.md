# PERCEPTION ATLAS — Body Systems

## Nervous system
Central target for perception, cognition, motor control and regulation. Atlas separates brain regions, spinal pathways, peripheral nerves and autonomic regulation.

## Respiratory system
Includes airways, lungs and gas exchange. Relevant factors include smoke, particulate pollution, infections and physical activity.

## Cardiovascular system
Heart and blood vessels. Atlas tracks vascular function, blood pressure, circulation and cardiovascular outcomes without reducing them to a single mechanism.

## Blood and immune system
Includes blood cells, immune organs and inflammatory regulation. Immune effects must be distinguished from nonspecific claims about «inflammation».

## Endocrine system
Hormonal signaling and regulation. Timing, feedback loops and tissue-specific effects matter.

## Digestive system
Gastrointestinal tract, liver, pancreas and related regulation. Nutrition, alcohol, medications and infections may enter this layer through different mechanisms.

## Reproductive system
Reproductive organs, hormonal regulation and reproductive outcomes. Developmental stage and sex-specific biology must be stated where relevant.

## Musculoskeletal system
Bones, muscles, joints and connective tissue. Physical activity, nutrition, aging, injury and systemic disease can interact.

## Oral system
Teeth, gums, oral mucosa and related structures. Tobacco, alcohol, diet and hygiene have distinct pathways.

## Vision and hearing
Sensory systems should be represented separately from general «nervous system» claims because their peripheral structures and exposure pathways differ.

## Cancer risk layer
Cancer is an outcome layer crossing multiple organs rather than a single anatomical system. Claims must specify site, exposure and evidence.

## Cross-system principle

One factor can affect multiple systems through different mechanisms. The Atlas therefore avoids a single «damage score» for the whole body.
