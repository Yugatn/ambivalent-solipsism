# SMOKING AND LEARNING

## Status

Evidence synthesis template. Specific claims about academic achievement, prenatal exposure, passive smoke and vaping require separate source-by-source review before being promoted to HIGH or MODERATE evidence.

## 1. Developing brain

WHO states that nicotine is particularly harmful to children, adolescents and young adults because their brains are still developing. Age is therefore a mandatory field for developmental claims.

## 2. Learning mechanisms

The learning layer may include:

- attention;
- working memory;
- executive control;
- memory;
- cognitive flexibility;
- reward learning;
- sleep and withdrawal-related concentration problems.

Mechanistic findings must be separated from observed educational outcomes.

## 3. Academic achievement

A correlation between smoking and lower educational attainment is not sufficient to state that smoking caused a particular student's grades or educational trajectory.

For every educational claim record:

- age at exposure;
- exposure intensity and duration;
- socioeconomic factors;
- family and school context;
- mental health and other substance use where relevant;
- study design;
- effect estimate;
- causal inference method;
- limitations.

## 4. Prenatal and passive exposure

These are separate exposure categories:

**PRENATAL EXPOSURE**  
**SECOND-HAND SMOKE**  
**ADOLESCENT NICOTINE EXPOSURE**  
**ADULT ACTIVE SMOKING**

They must not be merged.

## 5. Withdrawal

Nicotine withdrawal can temporarily affect concentration and attention. This is distinct from long-term structural or functional associations.

The interface should avoid the misleading sequence «cigarette → cognitive enhancement». Where supported, show the more precise sequence:

**nicotine exposure → receptor effects / dependence → withdrawal when nicotine is absent → short-term relief after re-exposure**

## 6. Evidence discipline

Do not use as completed conclusions without source verification:

- «smoking causes lower grades»;
- «passive smoking causes learning disability»;
- «vaping causes the same academic effect as cigarettes»;
- «smoking causes permanent learning deficits».

These may be investigated, but each requires its own evidence record.

## 7. Atlas presentation

**DEVELOPMENT → exposure → brain system → cognitive function → learning outcome → evidence → uncertainty**

The user should always be able to open the underlying source and inspect what was actually measured.

## Core rule

**Population-level evidence informs the map; it does not diagnose an individual learner.**
