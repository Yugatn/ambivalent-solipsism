# Independent Review, Appeal and Reversibility

## Status

Thematic representation of constitutional provisions. The full constitutional source remains in `BOOK/11_CONSTITUTION.md`.

Migration status: `COPIED`.

## Purpose

A subject should not be reduced to a single decision, classification, prediction or historical record. Where a decision materially affects health, autonomy, rights or development, the system should provide a path to review.

## Core principles

### Right to review

A subject should be able to request review of a significant decision affecting them, except where a narrowly defined and documented limitation is necessary to protect another subject or a critical system function.

### Second opinion

A materially consequential decision should, where feasible, be reviewable by an independent process or agent that did not produce the initial decision.

The second opinion is not automatically correct. Its purpose is to prevent one model from becoming an unquestionable authority.

### Separation of roles

The system should distinguish:

- observation;
- reconstruction;
- interpretation;
- decision;
- execution;
- review.

The ability to perform one role does not automatically confer authority over the others.

### Error and correction

The system should distinguish an error, disagreement, uncertainty, conflict, established harm and intentional harmful action.

An error should trigger correction where possible, not automatic permanent classification.

### Reversibility

When a decision can cause significant and potentially irreversible harm, the system should prefer reversible or reviewable measures where they can achieve the same legitimate protective purpose.

### Alternative path

A subject should have an alternative route when a decision materially restricts access to development, education, work, communication, services or other significant opportunities.

### Historical memory without permanent condemnation

Institutional memory may be necessary for accountability and learning, but historical records should not automatically become permanent evidence of present character, risk or guilt.

### Detection is not verdict

A signal, prediction, anomaly or risk score is not itself a verdict.

Automated detection may initiate review, request evidence or apply a temporary protective measure when explicitly authorized, but should not silently become final punishment or irreversible exclusion.

### Epistemic status

Every consequential automated conclusion should preserve, where practical:

- evidence;
- provenance;
- uncertainty;
- model/version;
- context;
- applicable rule;
- decision authority;
- review route.

### Self-validation prohibition

A system must not treat its own intervention as independent evidence confirming the hypothesis that justified that intervention.

### Subject non-reduction

A profile, reputation score, risk estimate, behavioral prediction or AI-generated summary is not the subject itself.

## Review lifecycle

1. Decision is recorded.
2. Subject is informed when disclosure is permitted.
3. Grounds and epistemic status are made available to the extent possible.
4. Subject may request review.
5. Independent review examines evidence, context and applicable rules.
6. Conflicting findings remain distinguishable rather than being silently collapsed.
7. Corrective action is applied where warranted.
8. Consequences are reassessed.
9. The system records the correction and its provenance.
10. The result becomes input for future system improvement without turning one error into a permanent identity.
