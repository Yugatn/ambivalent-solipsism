# State, Civic Participation and Distributed Governance

## Status
Thematic representation of constitutional provisions. The full source remains in `BOOK/11_CONSTITUTION.md`. Migration status: `COPIED`.

## 1. State as an instrument of shared development
The state exists to create conditions for subjects to live, develop, cooperate and resolve conflicts without making the state itself an unquestionable subject above them.

Public power is a function, not an independent justification for its own expansion.

## 2. Power must remain observable and reviewable
The greater the ability of an institution to alter another subject's conditions, the stronger the requirements for transparency, evidence, proportionality, auditability and review.

Authority does not prove correctness.

## 3. Civic participation
Citizens participate in public decision-making through elections, public feedback, lawful civic activity and other established mechanisms.

Participation architecture should preserve informed choice, access to relevant information, independent review and the ability to correct institutional errors.

## 4. Elections
Election rules belong to a distinct normative and legal layer. Any proposed eligibility condition must be tested for consistency with equal civic status, fundamental rights, evidence of necessity, proportionality and the broader constitutional architecture before being promoted to canonical status.

The earlier proposal concerning abstention from alcohol or other destructive substances is therefore retained as an unresolved normative proposal rather than silently incorporated as a settled constitutional rule.

## 5. Leader and institutions
The leader is not the sole source of information or correction.

A resilient system combines leadership, professional institutions, independent information channels, direct public feedback, verification, institutional self-correction and a defined possibility of leadership intervention.

The leader should be able to receive signals from below administrative layers, but this does not eliminate institutional competence or procedural safeguards.

## 6. Direct information channels
Citizens and professional actors should have lawful channels through which significant problems can reach appropriate authorities without being silently filtered by intermediate structures.

Direct access should not become unrestricted access to confidential data or a mechanism for bypassing due process.

## 7. Distributed sensing
Public reporting can function as a distributed sensing layer for the state.

Signals should be separated from findings, findings from decisions, and decisions from execution. A report does not establish guilt.

The reporting network must not become a system of total surveillance, political persecution or intrusion into private life.

## 8. State self-observation
The state should observe its own performance through independent data sources, audits, public feedback, professional inspection and historical records.

A dashboard should expose source, methodology, time period, uncertainty and regional distribution rather than collapse the state into one score.

## 9. Early warning
Multiple independent signals may increase the priority of verification. Increased verification priority is not itself a finding of guilt.

## 10. Institutional self-correction
Institutions should be capable of correcting their own errors without waiting for the leader.

Leadership intervention is appropriate for systemic failure, constitutional violations, major risks or persistent institutional inability to self-correct, subject to applicable safeguards.

## 11. Protection against information capture
Information channels to leadership require redundancy, access control, integrity protection, audit logs, conflict-of-interest controls and independent review.

No actor should be able to silently remove a significant category of information from the system.

## 12. Public memory
Significant signals, decisions, implementation and outcomes should be recorded where lawful and proportionate. Institutional memory supports accountability and learning but should not become unlimited personal surveillance.

## 13. Civil service culture
Public servants serve the state and society rather than an information-comfort layer around leadership.

Good-faith reporting of systemic defects should not automatically be treated as disloyalty.

## 14. Political neutrality of infrastructure
Public information and administrative infrastructure should not be designed to covertly manipulate citizens toward a political preference.

Institutional information should distinguish data, interpretation, uncertainty and policy choice.

## 15. Development impact review
Major public decisions should be assessed for effects on health, autonomy, rights, future development opportunities, reversibility and availability of less restrictive alternatives.

## 16. Separation of detection, decision and action
Detection can trigger verification. Verification can inform decision. Decision authorizes action only within defined authority.

No layer inherits the powers of another merely because it has access to its outputs.
