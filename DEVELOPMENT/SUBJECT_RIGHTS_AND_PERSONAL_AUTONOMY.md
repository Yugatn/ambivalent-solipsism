# Subject Rights and Personal Autonomy

## Status
Thematic representation of constitutional provisions. The full source remains in `BOOK/11_CONSTITUTION.md`. Migration status: `COPIED`.

## 1. Subject as the primary unit
The subject is the starting unit of the constitutional model. State, organization, market, algorithm and society are larger structures formed through interaction between subjects. Collective structures are real social mechanisms but do not automatically outrank the subject from which they arise.

## 2. Own model and right to change
A subject may form and revise their understanding of self, world and others. A person is not required to remain identical to a previous model, profession, lifestyle or social role. Systems should distinguish historical records from current subjecthood.

## 3. Right to exit
Participation in voluntary relationships, communities, information environments and systems should remain terminable where ending participation does not violate others' rights or applicable necessary legal restrictions. Voluntariness matters both at entry and throughout participation.

## 4. Personal space and property
Personal space, possessions and boundaries require protection. Access, inspection or intervention require an independent basis and defined authority. Security measures should not become a general license for unnecessary intrusion.

## 5. Movement and network access
Freedom of movement and access to communication infrastructure support autonomy and development. Restrictions require a lawful, necessary, proportionate and reviewable basis.

## 6. Development, education, work and rest
Development includes education, work, creativity, relationships, recovery, rest, acquisition of skills and other forms chosen by the subject. The system should protect the possibility of development without prescribing one mandatory life model.

## 7. Health and medical care
Health is a component of development. Public systems should support access to necessary medical care and reliable information while preserving informed choice and subject autonomy within applicable safety boundaries.

## 8. Relationships and identity
A subject may form friendships, love relationships and other voluntary bonds. The right to remain oneself coexists with the right to change. Neither development nor social conformity should be treated as a single predetermined trajectory.

## 9. Freedom of speech and expression
Freedom of expression is protected subject to the Law of Development. Criticism, disagreement, error, unpopular opinion, satire and criticism of government do not by themselves establish a violation. Restrictions require a concrete basis, necessity, proportionality, verification and reversibility where possible.

## 10. Sobriety and participation
Any constitutional provision connecting civic participation with abstention from alcohol or other substances must be treated as a distinct normative proposal and reconciled with the broader rights architecture before being considered canonical. This thematic node does not settle that question.

## 11. Information and official reliability
Subjects should have access to trustworthy official information, meaningful context, mechanisms for correction and independent review. Automated systems should expose relevant uncertainty and not silently convert predictions into facts.

## 12. State protection and fair treatment
State institutions should protect rights, property, personal space and access to lawful services. Decisions materially affecting a subject should have identifiable grounds and a path to review.

## 13. Right to choose consciousness and beliefs
The constitutional model protects the subject's ability to choose religion or non-religion, philosophical commitments and personal practices, subject to the Law of Development and rights of others.

## 14. Refusal of violence
A subject should be able to refuse violence and coercive participation except where a narrowly defined lawful duty is necessary to protect others and is subject to appropriate safeguards.

## 15. Infrastructure after contribution
Access to shared infrastructure can be organized through contribution, reciprocity or public provision. Such mechanisms should not become a hidden basis for denying fundamental rights.

## 16. Children
Children require special protection of health, development, education, safety and information environment. Protection should preserve curiosity, critical thinking and access to knowledge.

## 17. Subject non-reduction
A profile, reputation score, prediction, risk estimate or automated summary is not the subject. Institutional decisions must preserve uncertainty and the possibility of correction.

## 18. Review and reversibility
Consequential decisions require review routes, second opinions where feasible, provenance, epistemic status and reversible measures where the same protective purpose can be achieved without irreversible restriction.
