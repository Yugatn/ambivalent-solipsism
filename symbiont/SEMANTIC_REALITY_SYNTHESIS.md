# Semantic Reality Synthesis

## Core distinction
The phrase “reality is semantic” contains several different claims. This document separates physical reality, perception, conceptualization, language, meaning, social convention and interpretation.

**Experienced reality is semantically organized.** This is a safer scientific claim than **reality itself is made of semantics**.

## Scientific context
Semantic cognition is the capacity to use, manipulate and generalize acquired knowledge for language and non-linguistic behavior. ## AS model
For Ambivalent Solipsism, a useful formulation is:

**Reality is encountered through meaning, organized by meaning, and acted upon through meaning; meaning is not therefore identical to Reality.**

This extends:

**Model ≠ Subject.**

**Reality = Model + Residual/UNKNOWN.**

A World-Model contains representations of objects, relations, significance, expected consequences and social meanings. It remains a model rather than the world itself.

## Semantic layers
- PHYSICAL: physical processes and states.
- SENSORIMOTOR: signals and actions available to the organism.
- PHENOMENOLOGICAL: lived significance.
- CONCEPTUAL: categories and relations.
- LINGUISTIC: words and discourse.
- SOCIAL: collectively maintained meanings.
- NORMATIVE: values, rules and obligations.
- MODEL: integrated representation.
- RESIDUAL: unresolved or unknown.

## Meaning is constrained
Semantic organization does not imply arbitrariness. Meanings are constrained by bodily capacities, sensory input, environment, learning history, culture, social consequences and resistance from the external world.

**Interpretation is constrained by reality even when interpretation is not identical to reality.**

## Meaning is not truth
A meaningful interpretation may be false.

**Meaningful ≠ True.**

**Subjectively certain ≠ Objectively established.**

**Socially shared ≠ Physically fundamental.**

This creates an explicit error-correction requirement for AS.

## Semantic attractors
Repeated combinations of experience, language, affect, action and social feedback can stabilize recurring interpretations. AS may call these **semantic attractors** as a working model. The term is not a claim about a separate physical force.

## Language
Language can connect sensory features into abstract representations and acts as a social coordination tool. citeturn0search0

Therefore language can change the model through which reality is interpreted without thereby changing every physical property of the external world.

## Social reality
Money, status, roles, reputation, promises and institutional categories can have real consequences because people coordinate around shared meanings. This supports a concept of socially constituted reality without implying that the physical universe is created by language.

## First-person and third-person
First-person reports provide information about lived significance, relevance and experience. Third-person measures can provide information about behavior, physiology, neural activity and environment. Neither stream should automatically replace the other.

## Semantic measurement
Questions, scales, diagnostic labels and categories can affect how a person classifies experience. Measurement therefore can be part of the interaction rather than a perfectly neutral window into the Subject.

## Relation to YUGATN ORES
YUGATN ORES can serve as an observer-relative semantics layer: an event's significance is represented relative to observer state, context, goals, history and available information. This does not mean that every observer creates a separate physical universe.

## Epistemic separation
Semantic claims should be labeled as OBSERVED, INTERPRETED, SEMANTIC, MODELLED, SOCIALLY-CONSTITUTED, CLAIMED, NOT_TESTED, CONFLICTED or RESIDUAL.

## Anti-semantic-solipsism rule
The fact that access to the world is mediated by meaning does not establish that the external world is only a semantic construction.

**Semantic mediation ≠ metaphysical solipsism.**

## New principle: Semantic Reality
For a living Subject, reality is not only sensed; it is organized into meanings linking sensation, memory, prediction, value, action and social interaction.

## New principle: Meaning–World Constraint
A viable meaning model must allow surprise, error and resistance from the environment. If no experience could ever challenge it, it has stopped functioning as an empirical model of reality.

## New principle: Semantic Residual
When an event does not fit the current semantic model, it should not automatically be renamed to preserve the model. It should remain Residual/UNKNOWN until sufficient evidence is available.

## New principle: Semantic Development
Development can include restructuring categories, values, relations and interpretation. A semantic change is not automatically an improvement; its effects on health, autonomy, functioning and relationships must be evaluated.

## SymbiontOS application
An AI system should separate raw event, observation, extracted feature, semantic interpretation, confidence, alternative interpretations, action recommendation and outcome.

The system should be able to state not only what it detected, but also what meaning it assigned and which alternative meanings remain possible.

## Final synthesis
Scientific literature supports the claim that human cognition organizes knowledge through semantic representations, context, control, language, embodiment and social interaction. citeturn0search1turn0search3turn0search8

It does not establish the stronger claim that physical reality itself is entirely semantic.
